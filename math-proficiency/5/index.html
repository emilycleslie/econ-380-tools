Solving the optimization system

ECON 380 · Math Proficiency 5 · About ten minutes

Jump to: 1 Dividing the equations · 2 Substituting and solving · 3 The whole thing, with numbers · 4 The whole thing, with symbols

This page covers the content you need to pass Math Proficiency 5. Read the four sections, work the practice problems, then retake the exam. The section numbers match the exam's question groups.

Many times this semester you will end up with a system of three equations that looks something like one of these.

A

0 = 2XY − 2λ
0 = X2 − λ
0 = 12 − 2X − Y

B

0 = 2 − λY2
0 = 1 − 2λXY
0 = 128 − XY2

The route this page takes

As with any system of equations, there are several algebraic routes to the answer, and none of them is the only correct one. This page walks through the one we will use most often in class, because it works well on this particular shape.

1. Rearrange the first two equations so the λ terms are on one side and the X and Y terms are on the other.
2. Divide them by each other. The λs cancel.
3. Solve what's left for Y in terms of X — or X in terms of Y, whichever is easier.
4. Substitute that into the third equation and solve.

You do not need to know what λ is to do any of this. For now it is a symbol that appears in the first two equations and disappears when you divide them.

Here is system A, worked all the way through

System A

step 1 — get λ on its own side in each of the first two
2XY = 2λ      X2 = λ
step 2 — divide the first by the second
2XYX2 = 2λλ  →  2YX = 2
step 3 — solve for Y in terms of X
Y = X
step 4 — put that into the third equation
0 = 12 − 2X − X = 12 − 3X
X = 4,   and so Y = 4

Sections 1 and 2 below take those steps one at a time. Sections 3 and 4 put them back together — first with numbers, then with symbols.

1 · Dividing the equations Exam group 1

Steps 1 and 2. Dividing works because λ appears in both equations, so it cancels. What's left is a relationship between X and Y with no λ in it at all.

Worked example

0 = 2XY3 − 4λ,    0 = 3X2Y2 − 3λ
2XY3 = 4λ      3X2Y2 = 3λ
2XY33X2Y2 = 4λ3λ  →  2Y3X = 43
the left side collapses using the ratio move from Math Proficiency 1
6Y = 12X,   so Y = 2X

Common slip

Dividing the wrong way round and ending up with X = 2Y instead. Whichever equation goes on top, the same one has to be on top on both sides.

Try it: 0 = 3X2Y2 − 3λ and 0 = 2X3Y − 4λ. Find Y in terms of X

Y = X2

Dividing gives (3Y)/(2X) = 3/4, so 12Y = 6X.

2 · Substituting and solving Exam group 2

Steps 3 and 4. Step 2 left you with a relationship between the two variables. The third equation is the other thing you have. Put one into the other and a single unknown is left.

Worked example

Y = 3X,    0 = 20 − 2X − Y
0 = 20 − 2X − 3X
every Y becomes 3X, so the Y is gone
5X = 20,   X = 4,   and then Y = 12

Common slip

Stopping one step early. You solve for X in terms of Y — but the question wants X and Y in terms of the other numbers or parameters, not in terms of each other.

Try it: Y = X/2 and 0 = 30 − 4X − 2Y. Find X

X = 6

2Y becomes X, so 30 = 4X + X = 5X.

3 · The whole thing, with numbers Exam group 3

All four steps in one go. Here is system B from the top of the page. Notice that λ starts on the other side this time, and that it changes nothing.

System B

step 1 — λ on its own side; here it is already there
2 = λY2      1 = 2λXY
step 2 — divide
21 = λY22λXY  →  2 = Y2X
step 3
Y = 4X
step 4 — into the third equation
128 = X(4X)2 = 16X3
X3 = 8,   X = 2,   and so Y = 8

Common slip

Forgetting that the substitution gets squared too. Putting Y = 4X into XY2 gives X(4X)2 = 16X3, not 4X3 — the 4 is inside the square.

Try it: 0 = 3X2Y − 3λ, 0 = X3 − λ, 0 = 24 − 3X − Y. Find X

X = 6

Dividing gives 3Y/X = 3, so Y = X. Then 24 = 3X + X = 4X.

4 · The whole thing, with symbols Exam group 4

Often there are no numbers at all. In their place you'll see letters standing in for quantities nobody has pinned down — PX and PY, I, or Greek letters like α and β up in the exponents. The four steps do not change. Nothing here is actually harder; it only feels harder, because with no arithmetic to do you can't tell whether you're making progress.

Worked example — letters in place of the numbers

0 = 2XY − λPX,    0 = X2 − λPY
0 = I − PXX − PYY
this is system A again, with letters where the numbers were
2XYX2 = PXPY  →  2YX = PXPY
Y = PXX2PY
step 4 — substitute, and the PY cancels
I = PXX + PXX2 = 32PXX
X = 2I3PX

Worked example — letters in the exponents too

0 = αXα−1Yβ − λPX
0 = βXαYβ−1 − λPY
0 = I − PXX − PYY
step 2 — the messy left side collapses to something short
αXα−1YββXαYβ−1 = αYβX = PXPY
subtract exponents one base at a time — Math Proficiency 1 again
step 3:   Y = βPXXαPY
step 4 — substitute, then collect the X terms
I = PXX + βPXXα = PXX · α + βα
X = αI(α + β)PX
Try it: the same three equations as the last example. Find Y instead of X

Y = βI(α + β)PY

Everything swaps. At step 3, solve for X in terms of Y instead, and the same collecting step leaves Y's own exponent on top and PY underneath. The (α + β) is the same either way.

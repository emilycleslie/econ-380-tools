Marshallian demand ↔ Hicksian demand

ECON 380 · Magic Square · The equivalence between the two demands · About ten minutes

Jump to: 1 One choice, two descriptions · 2 The two arrows · 3 Why it works · 4 Worked example

This is a bridge between the two optimization problems: Marshallian demand holds income fixed; Hicksian demand holds utility fixed. With the right income or utility plugged in, they describe the same bundle.

1 · One choice, two descriptions

At the same prices, we can describe a consumer's choice in two ways:

Marshallian demand

Given income I, choose the bundle that gives the most utility.

Hicksian demand

Given a utility target U, choose the least-cost bundle.

The two bundles coincide when the income and utility are matched.

A matched example

At current prices, suppose a consumer with $100 to spend chooses a utility-maximizing bundle containing 10 units of X and reaching 50 utils.

Marshallian:   I = $100 → XM = 10, V(P,100) = 50
Hicksian:   U = 50 → XH = 10, E(P,50) = $100

Both problems describe the same bundle. The income and utility target are matched.

2 · The two arrows

To move from Marshallian demand to Hicksian demand, replace income with the expenditure needed to reach the target utility:

Marshallian → Hicksian

XH(P,U) = XM(P,E(P,U))
YH(P,U) = YM(P,E(P,U))

To move from Hicksian demand to Marshallian demand, replace the utility target with the utility attainable at the consumer's income:

Hicksian → Marshallian

XM(P,I) = XH(P,V(P,I))
YM(P,I) = YH(P,V(P,I))

3 · Why it works

Suppose we choose income equal to E(P,U). That is exactly enough to reach U. The Marshallian choice uses that budget to get as much utility as possible. It therefore chooses the least-cost bundle that reaches U—the Hicksian bundle.

In the other direction, start with income I and the utility it produces, V(P,I). The original Marshallian bundle reaches that utility. If another bundle reached it for less money, the consumer could use the leftover income to obtain even more utility. That would contradict the fact that the original bundle maximizes utility.

The two demand functions are not generally equal when fed the same third argument. They are equal when income and utility are matched through E and V.

4 · Worked example

For U(X,Y) = √(XY), the Marshallian demand and expenditure function are

XM(P,I) = I/(2PX)
E(P,U) = 2U√(PXPY)

Substitute the expenditure function into Marshallian demand:

XM(P,E(P,U))
= [2U√(PXPY)]/(2PX)
= U√(PY/PX) = XH(P,U)

Substitution changes the argument, not the underlying choice. It lets us use whichever demand function is convenient.

The takeaway

Marshallian and Hicksian demand are two descriptions of the same choice when income and utility are matched correctly.
